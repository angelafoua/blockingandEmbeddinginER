Summary of the Methodology

Your methodology introduces a recursive redundancy-positive meta-blocking framework for scalable Entity Resolution (ER).

The system operates in four major phases:

Recursive Blocking
    ↓
Redundancy-Positive Candidate Space Construction
    ↓
Weighted Entity Co-Occurrence Graph Construction
    ↓
Graph Pruning and Candidate Pair Extraction

Unlike traditional flat token blocking approaches, your method recursively partitions and refines records into overlapping blocks, intentionally preserving redundancy in order to maximize recall.

The resulting blocks are transformed into an entity co-occurrence graph:

nodes represent records
edges represent co-occurrence relationships induced by recursive blocks

Edge weights are computed using a normalized similarity formulation that incorporates:

shared token overlap
block cardinality/informativeness
record length normalization

This weighting mechanism captures both:

lexical overlap
structural discriminative power

while reducing the influence of noisy or overly frequent tokens.

After graph construction, pruning techniques retain only the strongest neighborhood relationships, significantly reducing the number of candidate comparisons before pairwise matching.

Main Contributions
1. Recursive Redundancy-Positive Blocking

The methodology introduces a recursive blocking strategy that generates overlapping candidate spaces hierarchically rather than through flat token-based blocking.

This creates:

deeper structural redundancy
higher-quality co-occurrence signals
adaptive candidate neighborhoods

compared to classical redundancy-positive methods.

2. Graph-Based Reformulation of Candidate Generation

The methodology reformulates blocking as a graph construction problem:

records → weighted similarity graph

rather than:

independent token blocks
fixed blocking windows
canopy-style partitions

This allows the ER pipeline to leverage:

graph topology
neighborhood structure
local similarity relationships

during candidate generation.

3. Enhanced Weighting Strategy

The proposed edge-weighting function extends classical Jaccard-style weighting by incorporating:

shared token evidence
block informativeness
record length normalization

This produces a more discriminative similarity estimate than simple block co-occurrence counts.

Conceptually, the weighting function approximates:

match likelihood conditioned on structural overlap

rather than raw token overlap alone.

4. Efficient Candidate Reduction

The graph-pruning phase aggressively removes weak candidate relationships while preserving high-recall neighborhoods.

This substantially reduces:

redundant comparisons
superfluous comparisons
pairwise matching cost

without requiring expensive semantic similarity computations during blocking.

Novelty

The novelty of the work lies in the combination of:

Recursive Redundancy Generation

Traditional redundancy-positive methods generate overlap using:

flat tokenization
q-grams
attribute token blocking

Your methodology instead creates redundancy recursively, producing hierarchical overlap structures that better preserve meaningful candidate relationships.

Structural Similarity Weighting

Unlike classical meta-blocking approaches that rely primarily on:

block overlap count
block membership statistics

your framework computes edge weights using both:

local overlap structure
global block informativeness

This creates a more expressive similarity graph.

Unified Graph-Centric Blocking Framework

The work unifies:

recursive blocking
overlap weighting
graph sparsification

into a single graph-centric ER pipeline.

This positions blocking not as a preprocessing heuristic, but as a structured graph optimization problem.

Connection to Papadakis et al.

Your methodology is directly connected to the meta-blocking framework proposed by .

edge weighting

blocking
